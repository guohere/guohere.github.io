Now that my personal website and blog are live on GitHub Pages, I want to start sharing short, honest updates on what I am actively building and learning alongside my job search and Master’s studies.

## 1. Building a German Learning & Coaching Web App with Google Antigravity

I have my **German B1 exam coming up in December**, and instead of only studying passively from textbooks, I decided to build my own interactive **German coaching website** using **Google Antigravity**—integrating AI directly into both my development workflow and my daily language practice.

The goal is simple: turn German preparation into active, targeted training rather than passive memorization:

- **AI-assisted workflow:** Rapidly iterating on features, UI, and study modules with agentic coding tools while keeping full control over the architecture and content.
- **Active skill training:** Practicing reading, structured writing (including realistic German workplace and exam prompts), and vocabulary recall in short, repeatable daily sessions.
- **Real motivation:** Improving my German isn’t just for the B1 certificate—I want to be comfortable collaborating in both English- and German-speaking engineering teams here in Germany.

## 2. Next Up on the Engineering Side: C++ and Rust

Most of my machine learning work so far has been in **Python** and **PyTorch**, along with **ONNX Runtime** and **ROS2** on edge devices. Next, I am planning to dive systematically into **C++** and **Rust**:

- **Why C++?** In Edge AI, robotics, and LLM inference, the performance-critical layer underneath Python is almost always C++—from **ROS2** nodes and **ONNX Runtime** execution providers to quantized inference kernels. Being fluent in modern C++ will let me go deeper than high-level wrappers.
- **Why Rust?** Rust is rapidly becoming a core language in modern AI infrastructure (tokenizers, high-throughput serving tools, fast CLI utilities, and memory-safe systems code). Learning Rust alongside C++ is a great way to build stronger systems-level intuition around memory, concurrency, and zero-cost abstractions.

3. Sharing small build logs and code exercises as I progress with **C++**, **Rust**, and new applied AI projects.
